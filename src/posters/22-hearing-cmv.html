B

Rescreen → diagnose → intervene

JCIH Year 2019 Position Statement, Interpretive Criteria · Outpatient Rescreening · JCIH Audiology FAQs (2024) pp. 1–3 · CDC Screening for Hearing Loss (2024)
Well nursery
  1. Initial inpatient screen.
  2. If needed, 1 inpatient rescreen, several hours later.
  3. Persistent refer → 1 valid outpatient rescreen of both ears, ASAP and before 1 month.
  4. Either ear still refers → pediatric audiology referral now (diagnostic ABR capability).
NICUAABR refer → pediatric audiologist for rescreen + diagnostic assessment; OAE alone does not clear this pathway. Very preterm + prolonged stay → diagnostic audiology before discharge, even after PASS.
Repeat screening raises the chance of a false pass; middle-ear fluid must not delay diagnosis. CMV collection proceeds in parallel C–D
Complete by: screening 1 month · audiologic diagnosis 3 months · early intervention for confirmed hearing loss 6 months. Programs meeting 1–3–6 should strive for 1–2–3 (diagnosis 2 months · intervention 3 months).

C

Who needs cCMV testing?

AAP Clinical Report, Care of the Infant With Congenital CMV Infection (2026), Key Action Statements 2 and 4
AAP: hearing-targeted + expanded testing. Test if any:
  • Initial newborn hearing-screen nonpass.
  • Maternal HIV, or maternal CMV infection during pregnancy.
  • Unexplained petechiae, thrombocytopenia, hepatomegaly or splenomegaly, elevated transaminases / direct bilirubin.
  • Compatible CNS findings or intra-abdominal calcifications.
  • Birth weight or head circumference < 10th percentile for gestational age.
PretermHearing screening delayed beyond 21 days → consider cCMV testing before 21 days.

Confirmed cCMV — arrange

  • Complete exam: growth / head circumference, neurologic findings, skin, jaundice, liver / spleen.
  • CBC with platelets · liver tests · cranial ultrasound.
  • Diagnostic pediatric audiology · ophthalmology exam.
  • Pediatric ID coordination. Abnormal neurologic exam or cranial ultrasound → noncontrast brain MRI.
  • Offer early-intervention referral to every infant with cCMV disease or isolated SNHL. Neurodevelopmental assessment at 12 months; also 24 months if treated.

Family · document · report · close the loop

ASHA Practice Portal, Documentation · Loss to Follow-Up (accessed 2026) · CDC CMV in Newborns (2025) · reporting is LOCAL
Explain:
The screen needs follow-up testing. We will check both ears. CMV testing has a short collection window, so we are arranging that now.
If cCMV confirmed: hearing changes can appear later, even after a passed newborn screen. Give next appointments, written contacts and family-support resources.
Before the family leaves:
  • Rescreen / diagnostic appointment: date, time, place, phone given.
  • Medical home, preferred language / interpreter, reliable contact confirmed.
  • Infant’s chart: each ear’s result, OAE / AABR method, screening dates, risk factors, referrals.
  • CMV indication, specimen type, collection date / time, result or pending status.
  • Named owner reviews pending results, contacts family / PCP, arranges confirmation, tracks missed appointments.
