15The sick newborn

A term or late-preterm baby who was fine and now isn’t. Recognize, stabilize, call, transfer. Delivery room 5 · A · early-onset sepsis (EOS) risk score 4 · EOS

A

Escalate nowAny one flag → peds/NICU at the bedside now; baby to warmer, monitor on (possible HIE: normothermia → F). Don’t wait for the next vitals round.

AAP Apnea (Eichenwald 2016) · AAP CCHD 2025
AHA/AAP 2025 Part 5 · AAP 2022 bilirubin

Red flags

Apnea
≥ 20 s, or shorter with bradycardia (HR < 100), cyanosis or desaturation. In a term baby it is never “normal”.
Breathing
RR ≥ 60 with grunting, flaring or retractions · gasping → B
Color · SpO₂
Central cyanosis · SpO₂ < 90% either limb · pale, gray, mottled
CCHD screen
Fail → C and echo (screen ≈ 24 h) 5 · D
Perfusion
Capillary refill > 3 s · weak or absent femoral pulses · cool limbs
Temperature
< 36 or ≥ 38 °C, or unstable despite a normal environment
Behavior
Lethargy, hard to wake · weak suck, poor feeding · high-pitched cry, irritability
Gut
Any green (bilious) emesis → E · distension · bloody stool
Neuro
Rhythmic jerking, staring, lip-smacking, apnea with eye deviation → F
Jaundice
Jaundice at < 24 h → obtain total serum bilirubin (TSB) now. TSB ≥ (exchange threshold − 2 mg/dL) → NICU 5 · C
Skin
Petechiae · vesicles (HSV) · after stabilization: cCMV indications 22 · C

At the bedside first 10 minutes

Call peds/NICU; assess airway, breathing and HRPeds / NICU attending now; state “sick newborn, needs bedside eval”.
Apnea, gasping or HR < 100 → PPV now · NRP 5 · A — position; suction only if obstructed
In parallel — assign tasks
SpO₂ right hand + a footPre- and postductal. Gradient or < 90% → C. O₂ to target B.
Glucose nowCheck point-of-care glucose. If symptomatic and glucose < 40 mg/dL:D10W 2 mL/kg IV then glucose infusion rate (GIR) 5–8 mg/kg/minPump rates 18 · 04 · newborn glucose 5 · B
Temperature · warmerAxillary target: 36.5–37.5 °C. If low: check glucose and consider sepsis. Don’t overheat; maintain normothermia unless the cooling center directs a monitored cooling protocol → F · HIE/cooling.
IV access · labs · NPOPIV · blood culture before antibiotics · gas, lactate, CBC, lytes as indicated. Distressed, bilious or lethargic → stop feeds, IV dextrose.
Clinical illness overrides every score. A passed critical congenital heart disease (CCHD) screen, a low EOS risk, or a normal CBC does not make a sick baby well.

Ask the history

  • GA, hours of life, resuscitation, Apgars, cord gas 3 · D
  • GBS, ROM ≥ 18 h, maternal fever / IAI, antibiotic timing
  • Maternal opioids, magnesium, SSRIs, diabetes, HSV, syphilis · suspected withdrawal, after stabilization 24 · I
  • Last feed, voids, stools, weight loss 10 · 02

B

Respiratory distressTachypnea, grunting, flaring, retractions or an O₂ need after transition: lungs, infection, heart — or not the chest?

AAP EOS (Puopolo 2018) · AHA/AAP 2025 Part 5
EPPVDN PPHN consensus (Heart 2016)

Support

  • Worsening, apnea, acidosis on gas or rising FiO₂ → NICU, consider intubation · NPO + IV dextrose.
  • Oxygen to the unit SpO₂ target; avoid prolonged 100%. Suspected PPHN: preductal 91–95% (EPPVDN).
  • CPAP for labored breathing or persistent O₂ need while breathing spontaneously, as in the delivery room 5 · A

Workup

  • Continuous SpO₂ (pre + post) · blood gas · glucose · CXR
  • Blood culture ± CBC if infection possible → sick = antibiotics D
  • Transient tachypnea of the newborn (TTN) should improve. Worsening distress at any hour → reassess for other causes.

Suspected sepsisIllness overrides the EOS calculator

AAP EOS (Puopolo 2018) · NeoFax 2026
UCSF Benioff neonatal dosing guideline
  1. Blood culture first — ≥ 1 mLSmaller samples may miss low-level bacteremia. Do not delay antibiotics to obtain the culture.
  2. Ampicillin + gentamicinCritically ill term baby: add broader gram-negative cover until cultures known (AAP) — NICU selects the regimen.
    InfantAmpicillin IVGentamicin IV
    ≥ 35 wk50 mg/kg q8h (≤ 28 d)4 mg/kg q24h
    34 wk, ≤ 7 d old50 mg/kg q12h4.5 mg/kg q36h
    Meningitis possible: ampicillin 100 mg/kg q8h ≤ 7 d · 75 mg/kg q6h ≥ 8 d, + cefotaxime, ceftazidime or cefepime per NICU.
    Gentamicin: HIE, asphyxia or poor urine output → longer interval (e.g. q36h) and levels.
  3. LP when stableNever delay antibiotics for it. Mandatory if blood culture grows a pathogen.
HSV riskVesicles, seizures, maternal genital lesions, high LFTs, CSF pleocytosis: startacyclovir 20 mg/kg IV over ≥ 1 h q8h(normal renal function; hydrate; pharmacy adjusts). Send HSV PCRs + LFTs, but don’t wait for them.
Usually stop at 36–48 h if cultures are sterile and there is no site-specific infection. Persistent unexplained critical cardiorespiratory illness → NICU/ID reassessment; longer empirical treatment may be justified. CBC, CRP and procalcitonin can’t diagnose or exclude EOS; lab abnormalities alone rarely justify continuing.

Any green (bilious) vomit → prompt neonatal assessment and call pediatric surgery now — not after the film. Bowel can be lost within hours.
Time-critical — in parallelAbdominal tenderness, distension or lactic acidosis → time-critical surgical review / transfer; agree destination with neonatology 23 · B. Unstable or peritonitic → surgeon may go to laparotomy without imaging. Decompress, hydrate, secure airway before any transport.
  1. NPO + decompressLarge-bore OG/NG (Replogle) to low suction.
  2. IV access · fluidsDextrose maintenance, bolus if poorly perfused · glucose, lytes, gas, lactate, CBC, type and screen.
  3. Upper GI contrast studyTest of choice in a stable baby. AXR first is fine, but a normal AXR or a soft belly does not exclude volvulus.
  4. Antibioticsif ill, acidotic or bloody stool D — culture first.
