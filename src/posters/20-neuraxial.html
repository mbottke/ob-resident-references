03

Platelets

SOAP Thrombocytopenia Consensus 2021

At any platelet count: bleeding history or DIC → avoid neuraxial procedures or consult hematology.

≥ 70,000
Low risk — reasonable to proceed (GT, ITP, HDP; no bleeding, no DIC)
50,000–70,000
Competing risks may justify it — anesthesia decides
< 50,000
Likely ↑ hematoma risk — reasonable to avoid
  • Thrombocytopenia of unknown cause and platelets < 70,000 → hematology workup first.
  • HELLP: platelet count within 6 h of the procedure or removal.

Before delivery · plan at 36 wk

OB → anesthesia handoff for every patient on UFH or LMWH. On prophylactic LMWH: consider switching to UFH 5,000 units SUBQ BID at 36 wk, sooner if early or urgent delivery likely. Therapeutic anticoagulation: individualized delivery plan that keeps treatment intensity.

Teach: skip the dose and call with contractions, ROM or bleeding.

05

Urgent delivery, hold not met

SOAP 2018 Figures 3–4 · ASRA 2025
Low- or intermediate-dose UFH, any timing
Neuraxial may still be right: in an urgent case the risk of general anesthesia can exceed the hematoma risk — placing without delay may be appropriate.
High-dose UFH < 24 h or abnormal aPTT · low-dose LMWH < 12 h · higher-dose LMWH < 24 h
Insufficient data — consider general anesthesia. Anesthesia weighs airway and comorbidity against hematoma risk.
Protamine only when UFH must be reversed urgently. Every unit keeps a written hold-and-restart protocol shared with nursing (SOAP). Higher-level care 27 · E 27 · F
