01

Monitoring

ADA 2026 §15 (15.8–15.13) · ACOG PB 190 (reaff. 2026)
  • GDM: fasting + 1 h or 2 h after each meal — 4 checks/day
  • Basal–bolus or pump: add premeal checks to dose rapid-acting insulin
  • Type 1: CGM recommended. Type 2 / GDM: individualize
  • A1c (type 1/2): < 6% if no significant hypoglycemia, else < 7%; runs lower in pregnancy; may check monthly. Use measured A1c, not GMI / estimated A1c
  • Pregestational: dilated eye exam in the first trimester, each trimester, to 1 yr postpartum as indicated

Nutrition · when to add a drug

ADA 2026 15.14–15.17 · ACOG PB 190
  • RDN-guided plan. Daily minimum 175 g carbohydrate · 71 g protein · 28 g fiber. Fasting ketones can flag severe carbohydrate restriction.
  • Lifestyle alone suffices for many with GDM. Targets not consistently met → add medication. Local metFORMIN first-line unless HTN, preeclampsia or FGR risk; insulin if targets still unmet, or metFORMIN contraindicated or declined.
Counsel before metFORMIN: crosses the placenta; long-term offspring data limited; insulin may still be needed. Document choice + plan.

Insulin High alert

ADA 2026 15.17–15.20 · ACOG PB 190 · PB 201 (reaff. 2025)
  • Required in type 1. GDM: add or switch when metFORMIN fails or can’t be used (hospital protocol). Type 2: preferred. MDI or pump; type 1: automated insulin delivery (AID) with pregnancy-specific targets recommended.
  • Typical starting total daily dose: 0.7–1 units/kg/day, divided (ACOG PB 190).
  • High fastingintermediate / long-acting in the evening (NPH at bedtime) High after a mealrapid-acting before that meal
  • Pregestational: needs fall early (first-trimester hypoglycemia risk), rise ≈ 5% / wk from ≈ 16 to 36 wk, often double the prepregnancy dose; bolus share grows.
  • Rapid, large fall in requirement → evaluate for placental insufficiency (data conflicting).

05

Oral agents in GDM

Hospital protocol · ADA 2026 15.21–15.22 · ACOG PB 190 · SMFM 2018 (reaff. 2024)
Local protocol: metFORMIN first-line for GDM metFORMIN dose / titration
  • metFORMIN: less neonatal hypoglycemia and weight gain vs insulin; 14–46% fail monotherapy. Avoid with HTN, preeclampsia or FGR risk. Preexisting use: may stop at end of first trimester; PCOS use: stop by end of first trimester.
  • glyBURIDE: more neonatal hypoglycemia and macrosomia; noninferiority to insulin not established.
Society positions
ADA
metFORMIN and glyBURIDE are not first-line, alone or combined: both cross the placenta and may not reach targets. Oral agent only if insulin can’t be used safely, after counseling.
ACOG
Insulin preferred; metFORMIN a reasonable alternative if insulin declined, unsafe or unaffordable. glyBURIDE not first-line.
SMFM
metFORMIN a reasonable, safe first-line alternative to insulin.
Stop GLP-1 RA / GIP-GLP-1 RA before pregnancy; other noninsulin agents not recommended.
08

Labor · after birth

ACOG PB 201 · ADA 2026 15.26–15.32 · ACOG CPU 2024
Type 1: never stop basal insulin (SUBQ, pump or IV) — even NPO.
Before IOL / CS
Night before: usual bedtime basal dose; individualize the morning dose.
Active labor
Glucose hourly, target ≈ 70–110 mg/dL; < 70 mg/dL → dextrose; rising → IV insulin per protocol.
After delivery
Placenta out → insulin resistance falls at once. GDM: stop medication. Type 1/2: adjust doses at once — early needs often ≈ 34% below prepregnancy dose, back over 1–2 wk; breastfeeding raises overnight lows.
GDM follow-up
75 g 2-h OGTT at 4–12 wk (or before discharge; less sensitive for IFG) 6 · A. If the OGTT is declined: A1c at 6–12 mo. Continue diabetes screening every 1–3 yr, lifelong; prediabetes → lifestyle ± metFORMIN. Breastfeeding lowers type 2 risk.
