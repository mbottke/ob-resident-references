01

Red flags secondary headache

ACOG CPG 3 (2022) · ACR Appropriateness Criteria Headache (2022)
  • Sudden severe headache: peak intensity ≤ 1 h after onset
  • Focal deficit, diplopia, field cut
  • Seizure, any BP → presume eclampsia: 2 · B now
  • Altered mental status, confusion
  • Fever or neck stiffness
  • Papilledema, visual change
  • Positional change: orthostatic headache stops being orthostatic
  • SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 · abnormal labs (platelets, AST/ALT, Cr)
  • New pattern, different from usual headaches

Check BP first

ACOG PB 222 (reaff. 2026) · PB 203 · CPG 3 · Severe HTN Bundle 2025
SBP ≥ 160 or DBP ≥ 110 recheck ≤ 15 min (sooner OK) · tell FM attending now
still severe → Treat within 30–60 minFirst dose, pick one:
  • Labetalol 20 mg IV over 2 min · recheck 10 min
  • hydrALAZINE 5–10 mg IV over 2 min · recheck 20 min
  • NIFEdipine IR 10 mg PO, swallow whole · recheck 20 min
Still SBP ≥ 160 or DBP ≥ 110 → next doses, rescue; below → monitoring schedule 2 · B. Target 130–150/80–100. Labetalol: not with asthma, heart block, bradycardia, decompensated HF.
SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 Up to 6 wk postpartum, headache + raised BP = preeclampsia until proved otherwise. CBC/platelets, Cr, AST/ALT, urine protein/Cr. New headache unresponsive to medication and not explained by another diagnosis = severe feature.
  • Magnesium sulfate High alert Severe features or eclampsia → give; new postpartum HTN + headache or blurred vision → consider 6 · B. Load 4–6 g IV over 20–30 min from a separate premixed bag, then 1–2 g/h on a pump; toxicity, renal dosing 2 · B.
  • BP falls after birth, then rises again days 3–6; preeclampsia can present up to 6 wk. Maintenance meds 11 · 02

04

Primary headache breastfeeding

ACOG CPG 3 · LactMed (NLM, 2026) · FDA labels
Complete BP, neurologic examination and red-flag assessment first → panels 01–02.
Step 1 Acetaminophen 1,000 mg PO + caffeine 130 mg (acetaminophen max 4 g/day from all products 19 · 04) or NSAID: ibuprofen 400–600 mg PO q6h (preferred NSAID in lactation) Ketorolac 15–30 mg IV × 1 is acceptable in the first 24–72 h. Breastfeeding: another NSAID (ibuprofen) after 24–72 h (LactMed) 19 · 04
No relief at 60 min Metoclopramide 10 mg IV + diphenhydramine 25 mg IV · may repeat after 6 h or prochlorperazine 10 mg IV slowly (≤ 5 mg/min; no bolus) Compatible short term.
Migraine Sumatriptan 6 mg SUBQ · repeat after ≥ 1 h if some response or recurrence; max 12 mg/24 h or 50–100 mg PO · if partial response, may repeat after ≥ 2 h; max 200 mg/24 h Compatible (LactMed); no routine pumping-and-discarding. Not with uncontrolled HTN, preeclampsia, CAD or suspected RCVS.
AvoidOpioids (codeine, HYDROcodone, oxyCODONE: infant sedation, rebound) · butalbital combinations · ergots (ergotamine, DHE).
ACOG cites expert opinion to pause breastfeeding 9–12 h after sumatriptan; LactMed: low milk levels, poor infant absorption, pausing only in extreme cases (e.g., preterm infant).
05

Post-dural-puncture headache

Uppal et al., JAMA Netw Open 2023 (multisociety PDPH consensus) · ASA 2021 · ICHD-3
Setting
Any neuraxial (epidural, spinal, CSE) — a recognized dural puncture is not required.
Onset
≤ 5 d after the procedure (consensus: suspect PDPH in any headache or neuro symptoms in this window).
Positional
Worse upright, often within ~15 min; eased lying flat. Frontal-occipital, neck stiffness.
With
Tinnitus or muffled hearing, photophobia, nausea.New diplopia or focal sign → imaging (03) + anesthesia, then consider patch.
Atypical
Investigate: onset > 5 d, nonorthostatic, fever, focal signs, high BP → work up as secondary (panels 01–03).
06

Treat PDPH

PDPH consensus 2023 (USPSTF grades) · ASA 2021
Epidural blood patch Severe, disabling, limits infant care, or refractory → don’t delay. Within 48 h of puncture: more likely to need a repeat. 15–20 mL, stop for back pain.
  • Before a patch, confirm all: no fever or sepsis; no local infection; acceptable coagulation status; anticoagulant hold before puncture 20 · 02
  • Second patch only after rethinking the diagnosis; before a third, neurology and imaging (ASA).
Supportive care
Conservative · 24–48 h Oral fluids (IV if not drinking). Scheduled acetaminophen + NSAID. Short opioid course only if these fail. No prolonged bed rest (VTE).
Caffeine · first 24 h Breastfeeding: ≤ 200 mg/day in total (ASA; consensus 200–300), incl. the 130 mg in 04 and drinks. Not breastfeeding: ≤ 300 mg/dose, max 900 mg/day. Oral; symptom relief only.
  • Not recommended: prophylactic patch, binders, routine triptans, theophylline, hydrocortisone.
  • Follow until resolved; tell the patient whom to call if it returns. Inform primary care.
07

Reopen the diagnosis every branch

PDPH consensus 2023 · ASA 2021 · ACOG CPG 3
  • PDPH loses its positional quality or worsens after a patch → urgent imaging: subdural hematoma, CVT
  • New focal deficit, seizure, confusion or fever → panels 01 and 03; post-neuraxial fever with headache → think meningitis
  • SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 on any recheck → back to panel 02
  • “Migraine” that is the first ever, unlike the usual headache, or not settling with first-line treatment → reassess for a secondary cause (panels 01–03)
  • “Tension”, “sleep deprivation” or “caffeine withdrawal” only after BP, neuro exam and red flags are all clear.
  • Two failed blood patches, or headache beyond 1–2 wk → reassess the diagnosis (06)
