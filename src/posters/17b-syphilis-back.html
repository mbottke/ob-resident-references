Penicillin allergy · shortage · peds ID

CDC 2021 congenital syphilis · special considerations
Penicillin allergy
Desensitize, then penicillin G. No neonatal skin testing.
ShortageScenario 1
Check penicillin G supplies; notify CDC/FDA. Aqueous penicillin G unavailable → use procaine penicillin G. Neither available → peds ID: ceftriaxone 50–75 mg/kg IV q24h over 60 min. Contraindicated with hyperbilirubinemia, prematurity through 41 wk postmenstrual age, or IV calcium in an infant ≤ 28 d old.
ShortageScenarios 2–3
Procaine × 10 d or benzathine × 1: apply the scenario’s single-dose criteria 17 · 02 (procaine if evaluation abnormal or incomplete). Single-dose ceftriaxone is inadequate.
Call peds ID
Any non-penicillin agent · maternal rapid treponemal + at delivery · persistent titers or abnormal CSF at 6–12 mo · desensitization.

03

Titer and CSF pitfalls

CDC 2021
Two separate examples · each infant titer compared with the same maternal titer
Mother 1:8at delivery · the baseline
Infant 1:16 example A1 dilution · 2× · test variability
Infant 1:32 example B2 dilutions · 4× · meets the Scenario 1 titer criterion
Fourfold = 2 dilutions (1:4 → 1:16), same test. One dilution is test variability.
  • RPR and VDRL titers aren’t interchangeable: compare mother and infant on the same test, ideally same lab.
  • Neonatal CSF needs a clean tap; 95% of healthy neonates have ≤ 16–19 WBC/mm³ or protein ≤ 115–118 mg/dL.
  • Treponemal tests stay positive for life in most treated adults; never use them to judge response.
  • Very high titers (secondary syphilis) can read falsely negative (prozone): ask the lab to dilute when suspicion is high.
