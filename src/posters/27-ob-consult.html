27When to call OB/GYN

Say what you need — phone advice, bedside review, co-management or transfer of care — and who owns care afterward.
Competence, privileges, resources and explicit acceptance decide the route. No national diagnosis list exists: response assignments are LOCAL.

D

Agree explicitly — who owns care

AAFP Consultations, Referrals & Transfers of Care (2025) · AAFP–ACOG joint statement §§A–B · workflow is LOCAL
PhoneQuestion, recommendations, reassessment, conditions that require bedside review. FM remains primary.
BedsideWho is coming, urgency / arrival plan, care while awaiting arrival. Confirm ongoing ownership.
Co-manageExplicitly agreed shared care: named maternal primary; OB/GYN responsibilities; order-writing roles; pending-result owner; escalation triggers.
OB assumes careAccepting attending, scope, effective time; FM’s continuing role; criteria for return to FM.
CurbsideGeneral discussion. If patient-specific advice will drive management → formal consultation, documented.
Beyond FM privileges → credentialed clinician; agree: procedure, one problem or whole episode. Procedure lead and maternal primary may differ — document both. A page or consult order alone does not establish acceptance; the sending clinician stays responsible until the receiver accepts.

Need MFM or a higher level of care?

MFM consultComplex maternal / fetal disease, suspected PAS, remote-from-term severe preeclampsia. Agree: advice, shared care or transfer.
Higher-level facilityCare exceeds surgical, anesthesia, transfusion, critical-care or neonatal capability → contact the receiving team. Suspected PAS: experienced level III / IV center. Severe preeclampsia < 34w0d, selected expectant care: appropriate maternal + neonatal resources (OCC 9: level III example).
While arrangingStabilize; continue indicated emergency treatment / delivery. Sending + receiving clinicians agree urgency, transport suitability, interim treatment; confirm acceptance, destination, bed / capability, transport team, equipment, records F.
Separate decisionsMaternal and NICU levels are separate assessments. MFM advice, OB/GYN primary care and interhospital transfer are separate decisions.
