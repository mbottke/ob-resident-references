Access
PIV for dextrose and medications. Emergency vascular access during resuscitation → UVC by a trained clinician; IO if IV access is unsuccessful or not feasible 5 · A Secure access and document position / confirmation.
Possible HIE → cooling center nowbegin ≤ 6 h of birth15 · F

After advanced resuscitation, assess promptly for encephalopathy: cord gases in depressed newborns (if unavailable, neonatal gas within the first hour or as soon as possible); repeat and document the neurological examination (serially if maternal medication clouds it); discuss borderline findings with the cooling center. Standard: ≥ 36w0d with moderate–severe HIE; aim to begin protocol-directed treatment within 6 h of birth. Passive cooling before / during transport only on the cooling center’s protocol, with close core-temperature and vital-sign monitoring; servo-controlled cooling is preferred. Selected 35w0d–35w6d or 6–24 h cases: specialist + family risk–benefit discussion, documented — call even when the usual GA or age window is uncertain.

Reassess until handoff

AHA/AAP 2025 Part 5, postnatal + postresuscitation care · S.T.A.B.L.E. 7th ed. · receiving-center protocol (LOCAL)
Unstable infant: continuous HR and SpO₂ monitoring; repeated bedside assessment.
  • Reassess breathing, perfusion, temperature and glucose; document response after interventions.
  • Record support / settings, infusion rates and trends.
  • Transport delayed → recontact the center; revise the stabilization and escalation plan.
