D

Mother: keep MOUD · treat pain

SOAP/SMFM/ASRA consensus (2025) Table 1 · ACOG/ASAM CO 711
Continue prescribed methadone or buprenorphine through labor, cesarean and postpartum. Verify formulation, total daily dose, last dose and prescriber / opioid treatment program (OTP).
  • Newly identified or untreated OUD / opioid withdrawal → prompt addiction-capable assessment and MOUD initiation plan. Avoid abrupt cessation or withdrawal-only treatment.
  • Naltrexone use → anesthesia / addiction plan ____; opioid analgesia may be blocked.
  • Medication for OUD (MOUD) does not provide complete labor or postoperative analgesia. Anesthesia plan early; offer neuraxial. Multimodal 6 · E · acetaminophen / NSAID maximums 19 · 04 Opioids on MOUD: individualized, not capped by 19 · 04.
  • After cesarean with neuraxial morphine: RR + sedation q1h × 12 h, then q2h to 24 h; SpO₂ / capnography as anesthesia directs.
  • With the prescriber, split the total daily dose every 6–8 h when appropriate for analgesia.
  • Stop OUD, with or without MOUD: avoid nalbuphine / butorphanol for pain or itch, and naloxone for itch — may precipitate withdrawal. FHR effects 29 · D
  • Naloxone remains indicated for significant respiratory depression or overdose (A).
  • No automatic postpartum dose reduction. Reassess sedation, breathing, withdrawal and cravings; individualize with prescriber. Review additional sedating medicines.

Plan of Safe Care

Begin prenatally when possible; update before discharge. Include infant care, maternal treatment / mental health, milk plan, safe sleep, unimpaired backup caregiver, locked medication storage, transportation and named follow-up owners.

CAPTA / CARA notification

Notification ≠ abuse report. Infant meets the state’s affected-infant criteria → required notification + Plan of Safe Care (box below). Notification itself neither defines abuse / neglect nor requires prosecution; an abuse / neglect report is a separate determination. Tell the family which is happening and why.
