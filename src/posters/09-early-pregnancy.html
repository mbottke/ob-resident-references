09Early pregnancy

Pain or bleeding before 20 wk: assess for ectopic rupture first; then determine location, viability, and how to manage a loss. Rh, nausea and vomiting, molar flags. ≥ 20 wk bleeding 1 · 01

A

First lookPositive test + pain or bleeding = ectopic until the location is known

ACOG PB 193 Tubal Ectopic (2018)
SRU First-Trimester Lexicon (2024)
!
Unstable, syncope, peritoneal signs, or hemoperitoneum on US → OR now (laparoscopy / laparotomy). Don’t wait for the formal scan.
  • Ectopic with hemodynamic instability → OB/GYN to the bedside for emergency surgery 27 · A
  • 2 large-bore IVs · CBC · type & crossmatch · hCG · call FM attending + OB/GYN now
  • Shock → massive transfusion protocol, 1 : 1 : 1 2 · A
  • No hCG is “too low” to rupture — ectopics rupture at low and at falling levels.
  • Rupture is still possible with an intrauterine pregnancy (IUP) seen (heterotopic) and after methotrexate.

Stable: workup

  • Minimum: transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) + confirmed pregnancy. Scan at any hCG level.
  • Quantitative serum hCG (baseline for trend) · CBC · ABO/Rh + antibody screen → E · Rh D
  • Speculum: os, tissue, cervical source, volume of bleeding
  • Live IUP + bleeding: no proven benefit of bed rest, pelvic rest or vitamins (PB 200). Prior losses → discuss vaginal progesterone.
  • Risks: prior ectopic, tubal surgery, PID, IUD in situ, smoking, IVF
  • Heterotopic: 1 in 4,000–30,000 spontaneous · up to 1 in 100 with IVF. An IUP does not exclude heterotopic pregnancy, especially after IVF.
Discriminatory level: most IUPs are visible by hCG ≥ 3,000 mIU/mL. If used at all, set it conservatively high (up to 3,500); multiples run higher. One value never justifies methotrexate or aspiration of a wanted pregnancy.

Methotrexate eligibility

Stable, unruptured, confirmed or high suspicion of ectopic, IUP reasonably excluded, reliable follow-up. Baseline: measured height + weight, hCG, CBC + diff, LFTs, creatinine, type & screen.

Absolute contraindications

IUP · rupture or instability · can’t follow up · breastfeeding · immunodeficiency · moderate–severe anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia · clinically important hepatic or renal dysfunction · active pulmonary or peptic ulcer disease · methotrexate sensitivity
Relative (lower success): embryonic cardiac activity · high hCG (> 5,000: failure ≥ 14.3% vs 3.7%) · mass > 4 cm · declines transfusion. Rise > 50% in 48 h also predicts failure.

Surgery (salpingectomy or salpingostomy)

Required: instability 27 · A, ongoing rupture pain, intraperitoneal bleeding, any absolute methotrexate contraindication, methotrexate failure. Consider: relative contraindications, patient choice, concurrent procedure. Salpingostomy if contralateral tube damaged and fertility desired → serial hCG after.
Counsel (methotrexate): no folic-acid supplements (stop prenatal vitamin), no NSAIDs, no alcohol, avoid sun; avoid opioids and gas-producing foods (mask rupture); no sex or vigorous activity until resolved; rupture warning signs. Contraception until 6 mo after the last dose (label; PB 193: some advise ≥ 3 mo).
Expectant only if asymptomatic, hCG falling or plateaued, accepts rupture risk (RCT, hCG < 2,000: 59% vs 76% with methotrexate).

No embryo ≥ 6 wk after LMP · empty amnion · yolk sac > 7 mm · MSD − CRL < 5 mm
Criteria are deliberately strict so a wanted pregnancy is never interrupted on a single scan. Stable patient + doubt → rescan; waiting is safe.
Say “cardiac activity”, “early pregnancy loss”, “embryo” — not “heartbeat”, “failed pregnancy”, “fetal pole” (SRU 2024). CRL ≥ 7 mm + no cardiac activity = embryonic demise; ≥ 11w0d = fetal demise. Workup for a cause after ≥ 2 losses (need not be consecutive, ASRM 2026); APS is the only thrombophilia consistently linked (PB 200).

Nausea & vomitingTreat early — prevents hyperemesis. Step up if no relief

Figure 1 algorithm
  1. First line
    • Ginger 250 mg QID · prenatal vitamin → folic acid only · P6 bands
    • Pyridoxine 10–25 mg PO TID–QID ± doxylamine 12.5 mg PO TID–QID (½ of OTC 25 mg tab)
    • or combination doxylamine 10 mg/pyridoxine 10 mg 2 tablets at bedtime; max 4 tablets/day
    • or doxylamine 20 mg/pyridoxine 20 mg ER 1 tablet at bedtime; max 2 tablets/day
  2. No relief → Add one of the following
    • Dimenhydrinate 25–50 mg PO q4–6h (≤ 200 mg/day with doxylamine)
    • Diphenhydramine 25–50 mg PO q4–6h
    • Prochlorperazine 25 mg PR q12h
    • Promethazine 12.5–25 mg PO/PR q4–6h
  3. Still symptomatic — choose by hydration
    Admit if she can’t keep liquids down despite outpatient treatment. HG: severe N/V limiting intake or activity, other causes excluded (often ≥ 5% weight loss). Check lytes, LFTs (AST/ALT usually < 300), TSH — transient thyrotoxicosis needs no antithyroid drug.
    No dehydration — add one
    • Metoclopramide 5–10 mg PO/IM q6–8h
    • Ondansetron 4 mg PO q8h
    • Promethazine 12.5–25 mg PO/PR/deep IM q4–6h
    • Trimethobenzamide 200 mg IM q6–8h
    Before adding to either branch: metoclopramide + a phenothiazine (promethazine, prochlorperazine, chlorpromazine) → dystonia, NMS. Ondansetron + phenothiazine → QT.
    Dehydration — IV 0.9% saline + KCl (lytes daily), then add one
    Thiamine before dextrose. Admitted with vomiting or poor intake: thiamine 100 mg IV with the first liter, then 100 mg daily × 2–3 d (Wernicke). Never delay glucose for hypoglycemia.
    • Metoclopramide 5–10 mg IV slowly, over ≥ 3 min q8h
    • Ondansetron 8 mg IV over 15 min q12h
    • Promethazine 12.5–25 mg deep IM q4–6h — not IV push
    • Dimenhydrinate 50 mg IV in 50 mL over 20 min q4–6h
  4. Refractory — still symptomatic after either branch
    • Chlorpromazine 25–50 mg deep IM q4–6h (10–25 mg PO) · keep lying down; BP for ≥ 30 min after injection
    • Methylprednisolone 16 mg PO/IV q8h × 3 d — no response by day 3 → stop; else taper over 2 wk (max 6 wk). Last resort; avoid < 10 wk (clefts)
    Ondansetron: low K or Mg, QT-prolonging drugs or long QT → correct lytes, ECG; < 10 wk individualize (small cardiac-defect signal). Weight loss despite treatment → enteral feeding first; no routine PICC.
G

Molar flagsSuspect → evacuate → follow hCG

NCCN GTN · FIGO 2025 · CDC MEC 2024
Think mole: hCG far above dates (> 100,000 = ↑ GTN risk) · bleeding · uterus > dates · hyperemesis · hyperthyroidism · preeclampsia < 20 wk · theca-lutein cysts (> 6 cm).
US: complete — heterogeneous cystic intrauterine mass, no embryo (“snowstorm”); partial — enlarged cystic placenta ± growth-restricted fetus; histology confirms.
StopDon’t biopsy a vaginal lesion after a mole — GTN metastases bleed.
Before evacuation: hCG, CBC, CMP, TSH, type & screen, CXR → suction D&C (US-guided), gyn oncology input · OB/GYN consultation 27

After evacuation

hCG q1–2 wk until normal (2-wk sample rises or plateaus → repeat in 1 wk). From the first normal (FIGO 2025): partial — confirm at 1 mo; complete — monthly × 6 mo; per gyn oncology. Post‑molar GTN: plateau (< 10% change) across 4 weekly hCG values over ≥ 3 wk · rise > 10% across 3 values over ≥ 2 wk · or choriocarcinoma / PSTT / ETT histology (FIGO).
Contraception: systemic hormonal MEC 1, any hCG. Intrauterine GTD suspected: IUD start MEC 4, keep MEC 2.
